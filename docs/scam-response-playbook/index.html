A scam-response playbook for impersonators, fake airdrops and phishing

Short answer: When a scam appears: remove the message, ban the account, warn members in one short pinned post, collect evidence, report it to the platform, and review how it got through. Prevention matters most: an official admin list, a "we never DM first" rule, restricted link posting and verification at join.

Step 1: contain (first 5 minutes)

  • Delete the scam message or link and ban the account.
  • If a raid is ongoing, enable slow mode, restrict new members or temporarily lock posting.
  • Screenshot first (username, ID, message, link) before deleting, for reports and pattern-tracking.

Step 2: warn members

Post a short, calm warning. Template:

⚠️ A scam account is DMing members pretending to be [name/team]. Our team will never DM you first, ask for your seed phrase or private key, or ask you to "verify" or "sync" your wallet. Official links are only in #announcements. If you clicked a link and connected a wallet, revoke approvals and move funds to a fresh wallet.

Impersonators

  • Keep a pinned official admin list with exact usernames.
  • Report impersonating accounts to Telegram or Discord using the platform's report tools.
  • Ask members to check the username, not the display name or photo.

Fake airdrops and giveaways

  • Announce real airdrops only in the official announcements channel and on official websites.
  • State plainly that you will never ask for a fee, a seed phrase or a "gas deposit" to claim.
  • Look-alike domains are the common trick, so publish the one official domain.

Phishing links and wallet drainers

  • Restrict link posting for new members, and use an anti-spam bot with a link blocklist.
  • Teach members never to sign transactions they don't understand, and to review and revoke token approvals using reputable approval-checking tools.
  • If someone was drained, don't promise recovery; point them to the platform, their wallet provider and local police or cybercrime reporting. Warn them about "recovery service" scammers who target victims.

Step 3: review

Within 24 hours, note how the scam got in (DM, link, new account) and change one setting to block it next time. Add it to your weekly health report.

FAQ

What should I do first when a scammer posts in my community?

Screenshot the evidence, delete the message, ban the account, and if many accounts are attacking, turn on slow mode or temporarily restrict new members.

How do I stop admin impersonators?

Publish a pinned list of exact admin usernames, tell members admins never DM first, and report impersonating accounts to the platform.

Can a moderator recover stolen crypto for a member?

No. Moderators should not promise recovery. Direct the member to their wallet provider and the relevant authorities, and warn them that fake recovery services often target scam victims.
