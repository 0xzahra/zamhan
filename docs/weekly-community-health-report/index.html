A weekly community health report template

Short answer: A weekly community health report tracks a few numbers (net member growth, active members, messages, questions answered, scams removed) plus short notes on what went well, issues and next actions, so you can spot problems early and show stakeholders progress.

Copy-paste template

WEEKLY COMMUNITY HEALTH REPORT: week of [date]

GROWTH
- Members: [total] ([+/- net] this week)
- Joins / leaves: [x] / [y]

ENGAGEMENT
- Weekly active members (posted at least once): [x]
- Messages: [x] (vs last week: [+/-%])
- Events held / attendance: [x] / [y]

SUPPORT
- Questions asked / answered: [x] / [y]
- Median time to first reply: [x min]
- Top 3 repeated questions → add to FAQ? [y/n]

SAFETY
- Scam / spam messages removed: [x]
- Bans / mutes: [x] / [y]
- Impersonators reported: [x]
- Any member reported losing funds? [details, no blame]

MODERATION
- Mod coverage gaps (time zones / days): [notes]
- Mod check-in: [notes]

HIGHLIGHTS / ISSUES / NEXT ACTIONS
- 1.
- 2.
- 3.

How to use it

  • Fill it in the same day every week; consistency beats detail.
  • Focus on trends, not single numbers.
  • Use the safety section to adjust settings (see the playbook).

FAQ

What metrics should a community health report include?

Net member growth, weekly active members, messages, questions answered and reply time, scams removed, moderation actions, plus highlights, issues and next actions.

How often should I report on community health?

Weekly is a good rhythm: frequent enough to catch problems, light enough to keep doing.

Is member count a good measure of community health?

Not on its own. Active members, answered questions and low scam incidents tell you more than total members.
