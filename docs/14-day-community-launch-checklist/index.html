A 14-day launch checklist for a Telegram or Discord community

Short answer: Spend days 1 to 4 on setup (purpose, rules, roles, bots, verification), days 5 to 7 on a soft launch with a small trusted group, days 8 to 11 on public launch with a pinned welcome and scheduled first events, and days 12 to 14 on reviewing what worked, fixing friction and recruiting moderators.

Days 1–2: purpose and structure

  • Write one sentence on who the community is for and what members get.
  • Pick the platform: Telegram for fast announcements and chat, Discord for many topic channels and roles.
  • Create a minimal structure: announcements (read-only), general chat, help/support, and an off-topic or introductions space. Fewer channels is better at launch.

Days 3–4: safety and tooling

  • Publish rules (see the rules template).
  • Turn on member verification or a join captcha to slow bot raids.
  • Restrict who can post links, images and @everyone mentions in the first days.
  • Set admin titles clearly and write down the official admin list; impersonators copy names and photos.
  • Enable two-factor authentication on every admin account.

Days 5–7: soft launch

  • Invite 20–50 people you trust. Ask them to try onboarding and report confusion.
  • Seed 3–5 good conversation threads so new members don't land in silence.
  • Test your scam response with a dry run (see the scam-response playbook).

Days 8–11: public launch

  • Pin a welcome message: what this place is, where to start, the rules, and "admins will never DM you first".
  • Announce on your main channels with one official invite link.
  • Schedule the first two events (AMA, community call, quiz) and post the times in UTC.
  • Have moderators cover the busiest hours, because launch-day spam peaks.

Days 12–14: review and hand-off

  • Look at joins, leaves, messages, and the questions asked most often; turn the repeated ones into an FAQ.
  • Invite your most helpful members to moderate (see moderator onboarding).
  • Start your weekly health report.

FAQ

How long does it take to launch a community properly?

About two weeks works well: a few days of setup and safety, a soft launch with trusted people, a public launch, then a review.

Should I launch on Telegram or Discord?

Telegram suits fast announcements and a single busy chat; Discord suits communities needing many topic channels, roles and events. Many Web3 projects run both, with one clearly marked as the official source of announcements.

What is the most important safety step before launch?

Lock down admin accounts with two-factor authentication, publish an official admin list, and tell members admins will never DM them first.
