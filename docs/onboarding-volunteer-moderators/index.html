Onboarding and training volunteer moderators

Short answer: Recruit moderators from helpful existing members, give them a short written handbook, start them with limited permissions and a shadowing period, use a private mod channel and a simple action log, and check in weekly so they don't burn out.

Recruiting

  • Pick people who already answer questions kindly and consistently.
  • Ask, don't announce: a private invite with clear expectations (hours per week, time zone, duties).
  • Aim for time-zone coverage, not a big number.

The one-page handbook

  • Rules and the enforcement ladder (link the rules template).
  • What to do in a scam (link the scam-response playbook).
  • What mods must never do: DM members first about funds, give financial advice, share unannounced information, or ask for wallet details.
  • Who to escalate to, and how.

Week 1: shadowing

  • Give limited permissions (delete, mute) before ban rights.
  • Pair each new mod with an experienced one.
  • Practise on 3 scenarios: spam raid, impersonator, an angry member.

Security

  • Require two-factor authentication on mod accounts.
  • Grant the minimum permissions needed; remove them promptly when someone steps down.

Keeping them

  • Short weekly check-in in the mod channel.
  • Public thanks, a mod role, and rotating shifts to avoid burnout.

FAQ

How do I choose volunteer moderators?

Pick existing members who already help others calmly and consistently, and recruit for time-zone coverage.

What should a moderator handbook include?

The rules and enforcement ladder, the scam-response steps, things mods must never do, and who to escalate to.

Should new moderators get ban permissions straight away?

It's safer to start with limited permissions and a shadowing week, then add ban rights once they've handled real situations well.
